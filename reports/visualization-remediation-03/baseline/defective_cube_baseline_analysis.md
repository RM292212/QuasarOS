## 1. Executive Summary

Forensic inspection of the baseline visualization subsystem reveals that the active viewport in `apps/web` does not execute the engineered WebGPU/WebGL2 raymarching renderer packages (`packages/renderer-webgpu` and `packages/renderer-webgl2`). Instead, it operates an ad-hoc, inline WebGL2 canvas context inside `OceanVolumeViewport.tsx` (lines 45–280) that renders a standalone, procedural rotating proxy cube with simplified raymarching.

While this baseline loads a low-resolution scalar grid from `GET /api/v1/analysis/volume-grid` (16 depth $\times$ 32 lat $\times$ 32 lon), it exhibits multiple critical rendering and scientific defects:
1. **Architectural Bypass**: Completely ignores the modular runtime, LOD brick stream, double-buffered staging textures, and WebGPU hardware pipelines.
2. **Ray Reconstruction Artifacts**: Calculates ray direction as `normalize(vLocalPos - uCamPos)` directly from proxy cube vertices rather than unprojecting camera NDC coordinates via inverse view-projection matrices, causing perspective warping during camera manipulation.
3. **Anisotropic & Vertical Scale Distortion**: Maps 50 non-uniform Copernicus depth levels (0.494 m to 5,727.917 m) uniformly into $z \in [0, 1]$, causing dramatic compression of upper mixed-layer gradients and artificial dilation of deep abyssal zones.
4. **Absence of Bathymetric Bed & Geological Context**: The ocean cube floats in void space with no GEBCO 2026 seafloor mesh, coastline vectors, depth tick axes, or orientation gizmo.
5. **Synthetic UI Mock Models**: Analytical inspection panels in `App.tsx` render hardcoded mock responses generated by synthetic formulas (`29.5 - Math.sqrt(depth) * 0.92`) rather than live backend query endpoints.
